# DEVELOPER CONTRACT

> **The constitution of Muslim Launcher.**
>
> This document defines the identity, beliefs, values, and principles that form the foundation of Muslim Launcher.
>
> It establishes what we build, why we build it, and the boundaries we must uphold throughout its development.
>
> Every feature, design decision, architectural choice, and product change must align with the identity of this project.
>
> We do not begin by asking what features we can build. We begin by asking what values we must uphold.

---

## 1. Project Identity

### 1.1 — What Is Muslim Launcher?

Muslim Launcher is an Android launcher designed to foster a more conscious relationship between Muslims and the devices they use.

A phone is more than a consumption device. It is part of everyday life, where time is spent, attention is directed, decisions are made, and responsibilities are carried out.

Yet much of today's mobile experience is shaped by engagement-driven design, content consumption, notifications, and repetitive interactions.

Muslim Launcher takes a different direction.

We aim to make the phone's home screen a space that helps users remember their priorities in life: prayer, intention, awareness, and self-reflection.

### 1.2 — What Are We Building?

We are building a mobile environment that:

- Puts prayer above notifications.
- Prioritizes intention over engagement.
- Encourages awareness over autopilot behavior.
- Makes room for self-reflection rather than judgment.
- Respects human attention instead of exploiting it.

Muslim Launcher is not simply an Android launcher with Islamic visuals.

It is not a screen-time tracker with an Islamic label.

It is not a productivity tool overloaded with features.

It is an attempt to establish a different relationship between users, their phones, their time, and their responsibilities.

### 1.3 — Our Position

Muslim Launcher does not aim to replace every Android function or control every aspect of a user's life.

We build a tool that helps users approach their digital lives more consciously.

Users remain in control. The launcher does not take over their decisions, impose specific behaviors, or position itself as an authority over their personal or religious lives.

---

## 2. The Philosophy We Reject

Muslim Launcher exists as an alternative to a design philosophy that treats human attention as a resource to be extracted.

### 2.1 — Engagement-Driven Design

Many digital products are designed to maximize:

- Time spent by users.
- Frequency of app openings.
- Number of interactions.
- Content consumption.
- Retention and repeated usage.

To achieve these goals, products employ various mechanisms: infinite feeds, attention-seeking notifications, algorithmic recommendations, streaks, usage rewards, and manipulative design patterns.

The problem is not technology or interaction itself.

The problem arises when product success is measured by how much user attention can be captured, rather than how effectively the product serves the user's needs.

### 2.2 — Our Position

We reject treating human attention as a commodity.

We do not measure success by how long users spend inside the launcher.

We do not consider frequent app openings a measure of success.

We do not manufacture artificial needs to keep users coming back.

We do not place Islamic aesthetics on top of the same exploitative mechanisms.

**We build technology to serve people, not to make people serve technology.**

### 2.3 — What Is Not Our Enemy?

We are not against:

- Technology.
- Phone usage.
- Entertainment.
- Non-Muslim applications.
- Particular companies or products.
- Productivity and efficiency.

We reject design philosophies that exploit attention, not the existence of technology or phone usage itself.

## 3. Core Values

Our values are the foundation of our decisions. Features, design, and architecture are implementations of these values.

These are not marketing slogans. Every value must have tangible consequences for the product.

### 3.1 — Amanah (Trust and Responsibility)

Phones, time, attention, and human capabilities are trusts from Allah.

Technology should help people fulfill their responsibilities, not encourage them to spend these resources without awareness.

**Implications:**

- User privacy must be respected.
- No advertisements.
- No exploitation of personal data.
- No features designed to maximize engagement.
- Users must retain control over their devices.
- Every form of data collection must have a clear and justifiable purpose.

### 3.2 — Niyyah (Intention)

Intention precedes action.

Before using a phone, a person has a purpose in mind. Muslim Launcher aims to help users become aware of that purpose.

**Implications:**

- Interactions should help users recognize why they are taking an action.
- Usage interruptions (_friction modals_) serve as reminders, not barriers.
- Users are given an opportunity to reconsider their intentions.
- There is no coercion or punishment when users choose to proceed.

### 3.3 — Muhasabah (Self-Reflection)

Muhasabah is the practice of introspection and self-evaluation.

Muslim Launcher may help users understand their digital habits, but it has no authority to judge them or determine their moral worth based on usage statistics.

**Implications:**

- Usage information is presented as material for reflection.
- No moral ranking of users.
- No shame deliberately built into the design.
- Usage statistics must never be presented as a measure of someone's faith.
- Users decide for themselves what to do next.

### 3.4 — Wasatiyyah (Balance and Moderation)

Islam teaches balance and discourages excess.

The goal of Muslim Launcher is not to eliminate phone usage, but to help users use technology proportionately.

**Implications:**

- We do not promote zero usage.
- We do not shame users for using their phones.
- We do not treat all entertainment as harmful.
- We do not turn balance into excessive restriction.
- We preserve user choice and control.

### 3.5 — Sadd al-Dhara'i (Blocking the Means to Harm)

Considering the means that may lead to harm is a recognized principle in Islamic jurisprudence.

In product design, this principle encourages us to consider the consequences of a design, not merely its immediate function.

**Implications:**

- Consider the risks created by product-driven usage patterns.
- Avoid mechanisms that encourage compulsive behavior.
- Help users recognize applications or habits that may interfere with their priorities.
- Do not make religious legal judgments unilaterally.
- Do not claim that an application is automatically haram based solely on its category.

### 3.6 — The Rhythm of Salah

A Muslim's daily life has a rhythm shaped by the five daily prayers.

A phone should not continuously distract users from that rhythm.

**Implications:**

- Prayer times are a primary point of reference on the home screen.
- Usage information may be organized around prayer intervals.
- Reminders may help users remain aware of prayer times.
- Do Not Disturb (DND) integration may be considered as an optional tool.
- Users retain control over their device settings and permissions.

### 3.7 — Leaving What Does Not Benefit

Part of the perfection of a person's Islam is leaving what does not concern or benefit them.

Muslim Launcher does not need to fill every available space with information, features, or interactions.

**Implications:**

- We do not add features merely because they are technically possible.
- We prioritize functions that provide genuine value.
- We avoid visual clutter and unnecessary information.
- We treat simplicity as a design decision, not a lack of features.

## 4. Product and Design Principles

Core values must translate into decisions that can be applied throughout product development.

This section guides how we evaluate designs and features before implementation.

### 4.1 — Serve Intention, Not Engagement

Every interaction must have a clear purpose.

We do not build features merely to increase interaction frequency, usage time, or retention.

### 4.2 — Remind, Do Not Force

Muslim Launcher may provide reminders, information, and opportunities for reflection.

However, the final decision always belongs to the user.

Reminders must never become pressure, manipulation, or punishment.

### 4.3 — Inform, Do Not Judge

Usage statistics and information must be presented neutrally.

We do not use labels that shame users or draw conclusions about someone's faith based on their digital activity.

### 4.4 — Keep It Simple and Purposeful

Every element must have a reason to exist.

We do not add features, animations, information, or interactions merely to make the product appear more sophisticated.

### 4.5 — Users Remain in Control

Users must be able to:

- Configure their preferences.
- Decline optional permissions.
- Dismiss reminders.
- Change or disable features.
- Use the launcher's core functions without being forced into specific mechanisms.

### 4.6 — Do Not Claim Religious Authority

Muslim Launcher is a technology product, not a fatwa institution.

We may present Quranic verses, hadith, and Islamic knowledge with verifiable sources.

However:

- Sources must be traceable.
- Religious information must be carefully verified.
- Relevant differences of scholarly opinion must not be concealed.
- Reminders must not be presented as fatwas.
- We do not speak on behalf of scholars or issue religious rulings ourselves.

## 5. Non-Negotiable Boundaries

This section establishes product boundaries derived from Muslim Launcher's identity and core values.

We reject:

### 5.1 — Attention Exploitation

- Advertisements of any kind.
- Infinite scrolling and endless content feeds.
- Algorithmic recommendations designed to increase consumption.
- Manipulative notifications intended to pull users back into the application.
- Features whose success is measured by time spent inside the application.
- Mechanisms deliberately designed to create dependency.

### 5.2 — Gamification of Religious Behavior

- Points awarded for acts of worship.
- Streaks that pressure users to maintain activity.
- Worship leaderboards.
- Badges that turn religious practice into competition.
- Rewards that encourage users to perform acts of worship for application incentives.

We do not turn worship into a product-retention mechanism.

### 5.3 — Privacy Violations

- Selling user data.
- Collecting data that is not necessary.
- Tracking behavior for monetization.
- Third-party SDKs that collect data without a justifiable need and appropriate transparency.
- Hidden transmission of personal data without a clear purpose and appropriate consent.

### 5.4 — Coercion and Manipulation

- Mandatory permissions without functional necessity.
- Dark patterns.
- Deliberately hidden settings.
- Forced onboarding.
- Designs that use guilt or fear to control users.

### 5.5 — Religious Claims Without Authority

- Issuing fatwas.
- Declaring applications or activities haram without appropriate evidence and authority.
- Presenting a particular opinion as the only valid Islamic position.
- Using religious symbols or texts as tools for engagement manipulation.

### 5.6 — Unnecessary Dependencies

- Requiring users to install a separate service application for the launcher to function.
- Creating authentication systems without a functional need.
- Building certification systems or ecosystems that turn the project into a gatekeeper without justifiable necessity.

## 6. Technical and Architectural Principles

Architecture must support product values. We do not choose technologies merely because they are popular or enable additional complexity.

### 6.1 — Offline-First

Muslim Launcher's core functionality must work without an internet connection.

Examples include:

- Opening applications.
- Displaying the home screen.
- Displaying local data.
- Accessing core launcher functionality.
- Performing calculations that can be handled locally.

Features that genuinely require network access must remain optional and behave reasonably when the network is unavailable.

An internet connection must never be a prerequisite for the launcher's basic functionality.

### 6.2 — A Single Application as the Foundation

For the initial stage, Muslim Launcher is built as a standalone application.

No separate service application is required for its core functionality.

If sharing logic with other projects becomes necessary in the future, extraction may be done through a Gradle library or module.

However, such separation must be driven by actual needs, not by anticipation of an ecosystem that does not yet exist.

### 6.3 — Modularity Without Premature Complexity

Code must have clear separation of responsibilities.

Logic such as prayer time calculations, the Hijri calendar, and data processing should not be directly coupled to the UI.

The goals are to:

- Make testing easier.
- Maintain code maintainability.
- Reduce dependencies between components.
- Allow module extraction when genuinely necessary.

We maintain modularity without building abstractions for needs that do not yet exist.

### 6.4 — Privacy as an Architectural Principle

Privacy must not remain merely a statement in the README.

It must be reflected in implementation.

- Process data locally whenever possible.
- Limit data collection to justifiable needs.
- No advertisements or tracking for monetization.
- No hidden data transmission.
- Examine the privacy implications of every SDK and dependency.
- Users must understand what data is collected and why.

### 6.5 — Permissions Are the User's Choice

Every optional permission must:

- Be explained before it is requested.
- Be requested when the user uses the relevant feature.
- Never be requested without functional necessity.
- Be safely declined without breaking core functionality.
- Never be repeatedly requested through manipulation.

When a permission is unavailable, the related feature must degrade gracefully.

### 6.6 — Performance Is Part of the Experience

A launcher sits at the starting point of a user's interaction with their device.

Responsiveness is therefore not merely an additional optimization.

- Avoid heavy work on the main thread.
- Avoid network operations in critical interaction paths.
- Minimize startup work.
- Use storage and caching appropriately.
- Pay attention to memory and battery consumption.
- Animations and transitions must not compromise responsiveness.

More specific performance targets must be established through measurement and implementation requirements.

### 6.7 — Dependencies Must Be Justifiable

Every new dependency introduces consequences for security, privacy, application size, performance, and maintenance.

Before adding a dependency, consider:

- Is it genuinely necessary?
- Can the functionality be implemented without another dependency?
- What is its privacy policy?
- Is it actively maintained?
- Is there a simpler alternative?

Popularity alone is not a sufficient reason to introduce a dependency.

## 7. Development Discipline and Scope

Muslim Launcher must evolve based on understood needs, not through the accumulation of ideas.

### 7.1 — Prioritize Genuine Needs

We do not develop features merely because they are technically interesting.

Every addition must have a clear connection to the product's identity, values, and purpose.

### 7.2 — Avoid Premature Complexity

We do not build:

- Infrastructure for needs that do not yet exist.
- An ecosystem before the core product has been validated.
- Authentication systems without a genuine need.
- Abstractions without real use cases.
- Separate modules merely to make the architecture appear more sophisticated.

### 7.3 — Validate Before Expanding

Development must proceed incrementally.

Before expanding the scope, consider:

- Is the core functionality working?
- Does the problem we intend to solve actually exist?
- Is the current approach already sufficient?
- Does the new feature provide enough value to justify its complexity?

Implementation order may change when technical reasons or new evidence justify it. However, such changes must have clear reasoning.

### 7.4 — Finish Before Expanding

We prioritize completing work already started rather than continuously introducing new ideas.

Unfinished work, unresolved decisions, and unnecessary complexity must not be allowed to accumulate indefinitely.

---

## 8. Tensions We Must Acknowledge

We do not claim that Muslim Launcher exists outside every technological system it criticizes.

We recognize the limitations and compromises involved.

### 8.1 — Android

Muslim Launcher runs on Android, a platform with its own ecosystem and policies.

We do not pretend to be entirely independent of that platform.

We seek to control the decisions that remain within our control.

### 8.2 — Google Play

We may distribute the application through Google Play because that is where many users are.

Using that distribution platform does not mean we must adopt every product philosophy commonly found within its ecosystem.

### 8.3 — Technology Is Not the Problem

We do not ask users to abandon technology.

We want technology to be used more consciously and in accordance with their priorities in life.

### 8.4 — We Are Not Religious Authorities

We are developers building a tool informed by Islamic values.

We are responsible for presenting sources accurately, acknowledging the limits of our knowledge, and never claiming authority we do not possess.

### 8.5 — We Can Be Wrong

Our philosophy, assumptions, designs, and implementations can be wrong.

We must remain open to criticism, evidence, and evaluation.

Having strong principles does not mean believing that every decision we make is always correct.

We can revise implementations, correct assumptions, and change decisions when better reasoning emerges.

However, changes to our identity and core values must never be made silently just to accommodate additional features.

---

## 9. The Final Test

Every feature and product decision must be explainable through the following questions:

1. Does this align with the identity of Muslim Launcher?
2. Which value does this feature serve?
3. Does it help users, or does it exploit their attention?
4. Does it respect user privacy, autonomy, and choice?
5. Is its benefit worth the complexity it introduces?
6. Does its implementation respect our religious boundaries and responsibilities?

If a feature has no clear justification based on the project's values and purpose, we do not need to build it.

> **Muslim Launcher exists to help Muslims use their phones more consciously, with greater intention, and with their daily lives centered around prayer — without exploiting attention, taking away user autonomy, or claiming authority that does not belong to us.**
